**The core frame:** "I'm not running away from my current role. I'm moving toward building software." Every answer should go **Past (what I learned) → Present (what I've built to prove it) → Future (why this role)**.

## Principles

### ✅ Do

- Link your current skills to engineering: SQL, working with data, troubleshooting, production support, following processes.
- Back every claim with evidence: projects, GitHub, anything you automated.
- Sound sure: "I made a deliberate decision", not "I thought maybe I'd try".
- Keep the switch explanation under 60–90 seconds, then steer to your projects.
- Show you've started already (months of consistent learning and building).

### ❌ Don't

- Criticise the current company, team, manager or the tool.
- Say your current work is "boring" or "I'm not learning anything".
- Make it sound like it's only about money.
- Apologise for your background; it gave you useful skills.
- Claim experience you don't have. Projects are projects; label them honestly.

## "Tell me about yourself" (60–90 seconds)

I'm [Name]. I have about a year of experience, first as an intern and then full-time, working on a data reconciliation platform (IntelliMatch), where I [configured data-processing rules / investigated data issues with SQL / supported daily production runs]. That gave me solid SQL skills and a habit of being careful about data quality and edge cases. Working with these systems every day, I realised the part I enjoyed most was understanding how software is built and automating things, so over the last [N] months I've been seriously building my skills in Java, Spring Boot, React and Kafka. I've built ShopSphere, an e-commerce platform that started as a Spring Boot monolith and grew into microservices with Kafka-based order processing, a React storefront, and Docker, Kubernetes and CI/CD. I'm now looking for a Java full stack role where I can build and own features end to end and keep growing as an engineer.

## "Why are you switching to development?"

In my current role I work with an existing product: configuring it, running it and fixing data issues. Over time I became more interested in how such systems are designed and built than in operating them. When I started writing SQL and small automations, I found I enjoyed the building part most. So I made a deliberate plan: I learned Java and Spring Boot properly and built real projects to make sure this is what I want, and it is. Development lets me create things and solve problems at the root.

## "Why switch so early in your career?"

Because early is the best time to do it. The first few years set your technical foundation, and I'd rather build mine as an engineer now than switch after 5 years, when it's much harder and costlier for both me and an employer. I'm not starting from zero either: a year in a professional IT environment taught me SQL, production discipline, working with deadlines and teams. Getting clarity early is an advantage, not a risk.

## "You have no professional development experience. Why should we hire you?"

That's fair. I haven't been a developer by title yet. What I can show is (1) projects that go beyond tutorials, using the same stack your team uses: Spring Boot, React, Kafka, microservices and tests; (2) a year of professional experience, so I know how real teams, processes and production systems work; and (3) proof I learn fast and consistently, since I built all this while working full-time. I'd be happy to walk you through any part of my code.

## "Isn't this a step back for you?" / "Will you accept a junior role?"

I see it as a step forward in direction. I'm comfortable starting at an associate or SDE-1 level, because the role and the learning matter more to me right now than the title. My professional experience should help me ramp up faster once I'm in.

## "You've only been in your current job about 6 months. Will you leave us quickly too?"

This move is the opposite of job-hopping. I'm switching career tracks, not hopping between similar jobs. I took my time to be sure, spent months building skills and projects, and I'm choosing a role I want to grow in for the long term. My plan is to go deep as an engineer here.

## "What from your current role helps you as a developer?"

- **SQL depth:** joins, aggregations and finding missing or mismatched records across large datasets.
- **Data quality mindset:** small data errors cause big problems, so I code defensively and validate inputs.
- **Edge-case thinking:** duplicates, partial data, timing issues, bad input files.
- **Production mindset:** SLAs, incident handling, root-cause analysis, clear communication during issues.
- **Teamwork & process:** working with other teams, tickets, deadlines and documentation.

## "How do you know you'll enjoy development?"

I tested it before deciding. I built ShopSphere step by step from a plain Java library to a deployed microservices platform, including the hard parts: debugging Kafka consumers, handling concurrent orders without overselling, writing tests and setting up Docker and CI. I enjoyed even the frustrating parts, and I kept going consistently for [N] months alongside a full-time job. That's what convinced me.

## Questions to ask the interviewer

- What does the first 3 months look like for a new engineer on this team?
- What's your tech stack and how do you deploy (CI/CD, cloud, Kubernetes)?
- How are code reviews and mentoring done for junior engineers?
- What's a recent technical challenge the team solved?
- How do you measure success for someone in this role after 6 months?

## Salary and notice period answers

- **Expected CTC:** "Based on my research for this role and my experience, I'm looking at a range of X–Y. I'm flexible for the right learning opportunity." (Research the range first.)
- **Notice period:** state it plainly, and mention whether it can be bought out or shortened.
- **Current CTC:** answer honestly. Don't inflate it; it gets checked against payslips.
